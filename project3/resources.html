Backlog Resources

Templates, tools, methods, and reference materials for creating clearer backlog items.

Why resources matter

A strong backlog process depends on repeatable habits. Templates, checklists, and shared terminology help a Product Owner communicate more clearly with stakeholders, developers, designers, testers, and business partners.

The resources below can be used before backlog refinement, during planning conversations, or when a request needs more discovery before it is ready for development.

Templates and checklists

These resources help turn vague requests into specific, testable, and prioritized backlog items.

Stakeholder interview questions

Use these questions before writing the backlog item.

  • Who is affected by this problem?
  • What happens today without this change?
  • What business result should improve?
  • What deadline, risk, or dependency exists?

User story template

Use this format when the work is centered on a user need.

As a user type, I want a capability, so that I can achieve a specific outcome.

Acceptance criteria checklist

Use this checklist to confirm that the expected behavior is clear.

  • Can the behavior be tested?
  • Are success and failure cases included?
  • Are error messages or edge cases described?
  • Is the expected result visible to the team?

Definition of Ready checklist

Use this before an item is pulled into development.

  • The user or business problem is clear.
  • The acceptance criteria are written.
  • The priority is understood.
  • Major dependencies are documented.

Backlog refinement agenda

Use this structure to keep refinement meetings focused.

  1. Review the goal of the item.
  2. Clarify open questions.
  3. Discuss dependencies and risks.
  4. Confirm acceptance criteria.
  5. Decide whether the item is ready.

Priority scorecard

Use a simple scorecard when several requests compete for the same development capacity.

  • Business value
  • User impact
  • Urgency
  • Risk reduction
  • Estimated effort

Prioritization methods

Prioritization helps the Product Owner decide what should be discussed, refined, and delivered first. The best method depends on the team, the product, and the type of decision being made.

Common methods for prioritizing backlog work
Method Best for How it helps
MoSCoW Scope conversations Separates work into Must Have, Should Have, Could Have, and Won't Have categories.
Value vs. Effort Small to medium backlogs Compares expected value against estimated effort to identify quick wins and larger investments.
RICE Feature prioritization Scores items using reach, impact, confidence, and effort.
Cost of Delay Time-sensitive work Highlights the impact of waiting to deliver a feature, fix, or process improvement.
Risk reduction Unclear or technical work Gives priority to work that reduces uncertainty, validates an assumption, or removes a blocker.

Tools and further reading

A backlog can be managed in many tools. In my Product Owner workflow, Microsoft Planner in Teams was a practical starting point because it kept backlog items, ownership, due dates, and status updates close to the team’s daily communication.

The important part is not the tool itself, but whether the team uses it consistently to communicate scope, priority, status, dependencies, and decisions.

  • Microsoft Planner in Teams — useful for organizing backlog items as tasks, assigning owners, tracking due dates, and keeping work visible inside the team’s collaboration space.
    • Jira — commonly used to manage epics, user stories, bugs, sprints, and releases.
  • Azure DevOps — often used by IT teams for boards, repositories, pipelines, and work items.
  • Trello — useful for lightweight boards, simple workflows, and visual task tracking.
  • Miro — useful for mapping processes, brainstorming ideas, and organizing discovery notes.
  • Confluence — commonly used for team documentation, decisions, meeting notes, and product requirements.
  • The Scrum Guide — a reference for Scrum roles, events, artifacts, and commitments.
  • Atlassian Agile resources — articles and guides about agile practices, product work, and team collaboration.

Backlog glossary

Product and development teams use many specialized terms. A shared glossary helps reduce confusion during planning and refinement.

Epic
A large body of work that is usually too broad to complete in one development cycle.
Feature
A capability or group of related functionality that supports a business or user goal.
User story
A backlog item written from the perspective of a user need and desired outcome.
Acceptance criteria
Conditions that describe what must be true for a backlog item to be accepted as complete.
Backlog refinement
A team activity where backlog items are reviewed, clarified, split, estimated, or reprioritized.
Definition of Ready
A shared standard for deciding whether a backlog item has enough information to be considered ready for development.
Spike
A time-boxed investigation used when the team needs to reduce uncertainty before implementation.
Dependency
A relationship where one item depends on another task, team, system, decision, or external condition.

How to use these resources

  1. Start with stakeholder questions to understand the request.
  2. Write the item using a clear user story or requirement format.
  3. Add acceptance criteria that can be tested.
  4. Use a prioritization method when multiple items compete for attention.
  5. Review the item with the IT team before it moves into development.
